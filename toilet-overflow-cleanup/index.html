Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Missoula: What to Know

It is tempting to grab a mop after a toilet overflows, yet the water never stays on top of the tile. Some slips beneath the baseboard and into the wall, and some drops through the opening around the drain pipe. What matters most is what was in it. Water from the tank or supply line is clean. Bowl water is not clean, so we handle it as contaminated from the start.

The first thing our Missoula team does is identify the water category, since every later step depends on it. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. Gray or black water means protective gear, removal of soaked porous materials like carpet pad, and disinfection of every surface it touched. Then we check the subfloor, the wall bases and the room underneath with moisture meters so nothing is left wet.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the SupplyFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float to stop the fill.
  2. Stop FlushingDo not flush again or run sinks, showers or the washer. A blockage in the drain line means water sent down any fixture can back up.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterBowl water, or any water with waste in it, means kids and pets stay out of the room. Should you have to cross it, wear gloves and waterproof footwear.
  4. Check the Room BelowWith a second floor bathroom, inspect the ceiling of the room beneath. If it is dripping or sagging, switch off power to that room and move belongings out of the way.
  5. Block the SpreadOld towels can stop clean water before it gets to a hallway or carpet. Contaminated water should be left to a crew that has the right disinfectants and equipment.
Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Too much paper, wipes or a child's toy flushed and lodged in the trap.
  • Blockage in the main sewer line, which pushes sewage back out at the lowest toilet.
  • Septic systems that are full or drain fields that are waterlogged, which send water back toward the house.
  • Roots growing into the sewer line, or a section that has collapsed, slowing all the drains.
  • A clog in the trap or drain line that sends bowl water over the rim.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Wet Vanity and Wall Bases

Water spreads to the vanity toe kick and the lower edge of the drywall, where it wicks upward. Several inches of wall above floor level can hold moisture with no visible sign on the paint.

Subfloor Saturation

Water gets beneath tile and vinyl through grout lines and the toilet base. The plywood underneath swells, delaminates and can loosen the flooring bonded to it.

Odor That Returns

When contaminated water dries under flooring or in the base of a wall, the smell comes back in humid weather and stays until that material is removed.

Bacteria in Building Materials

Bowl and drain water leaves bacteria in carpet, pad, baseboards and drywall. Porous materials cannot be reliably disinfected and usually have to be removed.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

A sudden and accidental overflow, for example from an unexpected clog or a fill valve that failed, is covered by many homeowners policies. Sewer or drain backup is often excluded without a backup endorsement on the policy, and an overflow from a blocked main line can fall under that exclusion. If the toilet leaked at its base for months, that damage is usually not covered. Read your policy or call your agent to find out which situation applies to you.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • What the bathroom floor is made of, and if it has to be removed or can be dried.
  • If the bathroom is on an upper floor, the wet ceiling or walls underneath add to the job.
  • How long the water sat before extraction and cleanup began.
  • Whether the toilet or vanity has to be pulled to dry and clean beneath it.
