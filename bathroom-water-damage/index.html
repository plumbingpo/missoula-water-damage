Bathroom Water Damage: What Missoula Homeowners Should Know

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. Our Missoula team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Warning Signs

  • A hollow sound, movement, or loose tiles in the floor by the bathtub
  • The bottom panel inside the vanity has swelled, stained or bowed
  • Caulk along the tub or shower that is cracked, peeling, or black with mold
  • Hours after anyone showered, the grout is still dark with moisture
  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • Baseboard puffing up or paint coming off near the shower wall
What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

The Room Below

The stain on the lower ceiling can be several feet off from the leak itself, because water travels along pipes and joists first. Wiring and light fixtures may lie along that route.

Slow Vanity Drips

Months can pass with a supply valve or drain joint dripping, hidden by whatever is stored in the vanity. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

A Failed Shower Pan

A cracked pan or liner lets water out under the shower floor every time it is used. Framing under the shower stays saturated even though the surface shows no flaw.

Rotted Floor at the Toilet

A failed wax ring leaks a little with every flush. It is category 3 water, and the wood surrounding the flange decays until the toilet wobbles.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Open What Is Needed

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

The first few days

Water Slips Through

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months or more

Structure Weakens

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.
