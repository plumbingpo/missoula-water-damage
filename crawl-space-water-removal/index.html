Crawl Space Water Removal Help in Missoula

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. By then the insulation is usually soaked and the wood above it has been wet for days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

What It Looks Like

  • Puddles or wet muck visible just inside the crawl space door
  • More crickets, roaches or other bugs showing up near the floor level
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • Corroded metal such as duct straps, pipe hangers and steel posts
  • Fuzzy patches, pale or dark, spreading across the framing or subfloor
  • Tide lines or white mineral crust on the piers and foundation walls

How We Fix It

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Inspect and Make Safe

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Pump Out Water

We run pumps and extraction lines until the pooled water is gone, low pockets and trenches along the footings included. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Dry and Verify

Compact fans and dehumidification work in the crawl space as long as it takes for the framing to test normal. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Weakened Floor Framing

Rot often starts on top of the joist or at the sill plate, where you cannot see it from the hatch. We probe the wood to find soft spots.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.

Contaminated Water

What collects beneath a home is frequently dirty. A leaking drain line or groundwater carrying soil and waste is Category 2 or 3 and needs protective gear and disinfection.

What You Breathe Upstairs

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

Day one

Puddles Form Underneath

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Mold can start on damp joists and paper faced insulation. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. The lumber goes on taking in moisture, a stale smell spreads and flooring can begin to cup.

After a week

Wood Starts Rotting

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.
