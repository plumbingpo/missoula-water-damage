Ceiling Water Damage Repair: What Missoula Homeowners Should Know

The stain overhead comes at the end of the story, not the beginning. By the time the stain appears, water has already run along a joist, soaked the insulation and pooled on the back of the drywall. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. Nothing is repaired until the origin of the water has been tracked down.

Painting over the stain does not fix it. While the space overhead stays damp, the stain returns, and within 24 to 48 hours mold can begin on the drywall paper. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Do not stand beneath it, and get help on the way.

What It Looks Like

  • Dark spots or speckled growth forming on the ceiling surface
  • Joint tape coming loose, with screws and panel edges telegraphing through
  • Drip marks at the top of a wall where it meets the ceiling
  • Hairline cracks spreading outward from a stained or damp spot
  • Discolored rings overhead that get bigger each time it rains or the bathroom is used
  • One room that smells damp even though nothing looks wet
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Growth on the Hidden Side

The top side of the drywall and the insulation resting on it stay wet long after the surface feels dry, which is where mold tends to grow unseen.

The Leak Is Somewhere Else

The wet spot is often not below the leak, since water rides pipes, ducts and joists before it falls. Opening the ceiling only at the stain can miss wet material farther along.

Electricity Overhead

The damp area usually includes fan mounts, recessed fixtures and wiring boxes. Turn off that room's breaker, because a wet fixture may short out.

Sudden Collapse

Water adds a lot of weight to a panel while weakening its hold on the framing. A bulge can drop a whole section onto whatever is underneath.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Source

We track the water back to where it starts using moisture meters and thermal imaging, checking the room or attic above. Repairs are pointless until that leak is shut down.

2

Secure the Room

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. If the ceiling is holding water, we release it in a controlled way and catch it.

3

Remove and Dry Out

Wet insulation is removed along with any drywall that cannot stay. Drying equipment, meaning a dehumidifier and air movers, works on the framing and adjacent drywall, with antimicrobial treatment for water that was not clean.

4

Repair and Finish

When everything reads dry, our crew replaces the insulation, hangs drywall, finishes the seams, blends the texture with the rest of the ceiling, then applies stain blocker and paint.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Spreads Above

Water runs along joists and soaks into insulation. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

Hours 24 to 48

Stains and Bubbles

Discoloration shows up and the paint begins bubbling. The paper layer becomes a place mold is able to grow, and heavy wet insulation bears down from above.

Day 3 to 7

The Ceiling Bows

The panel turns soft and starts slipping off its fasteners. Between the joists the surface dips, joints split apart and a musty smell arrives below.

After a week

Sections May Fall

Saturated pieces may drop and carry soaked insulation down too. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.
